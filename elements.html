How Our Hidden Markov Model Works

We built this model to figure out what "mode" the market is in. Here's the reasoning behind it, explained the way we'd explain it to each other.

What a Hidden Markov Model actually does

You can't directly observe what "regime" the market is in. There's no ticker for it. What you can observe is price behavior: returns, volatility, how choppy things feel day to day. A Hidden Markov Model takes that observable data and works backward to estimate the hidden state that's probably producing it.

So instead of us deciding "the market looks trendy this week" based on a chart, the model assigns probabilities to a handful of underlying states and updates them as new data comes in. It's a statistical way of answering the question every trader asks anyway: what kind of market am I actually in right now?

Why regime matters

A trend-following strategy that prints money in March can bleed you dry in June, and it's not because the strategy got worse. The market changed underneath it. Trend-following needs a trend. Mean-reversion needs the opposite: a range-bound, well-behaved market where things snap back after they stretch too far.

Most people figure out which regime they're in after the fact, once the drawdown makes it obvious. The point of the HMM is to give us that read earlier, using returns and volatility as the inputs, so we can size or switch strategies before the regime shift shows up in our P&L.

The intuition, without the math

The model assumes the market is always sitting in one of a few hidden states, and that each state has its own personality. One might be quiet and low-volatility. Another might be sharply trending. A third might be the kind of chaotic, whipsaw environment where nothing behaves.

Feed it a history of returns and it answers three questions at once: which state we're probably in right now, how likely we are to flip into a different one soon, and what returns typically look like once we're there. Run that same question every day and you get a rolling picture of how the market drifts between regimes over time, not a single snapshot but a path.

The three states, in practice

In our current build, the model settles on three states that roughly map to conditions any trader would recognize:

  • Stable, low volatility: quiet, grinding markets
  • Trending, directional: clear momentum, one way or the other
  • High volatility, chaotic: the kind of tape that chews up both trend and mean-reversion strategies

We didn't hand-label these. The model finds them on its own by clustering how returns behave, which is the whole point: it adapts to whatever the data actually looks like instead of us imposing a definition of "trending" that might be wrong.

Training it

We train the HMM on historical price data, and in that process it works out three things at once: how often each state tends to show up, how likely the market is to jump from one state to another, and what returns and volatility typically look like once you're inside a given state.

After that, it's fast. New data comes in, and the model updates its read on the current regime almost immediately.

Reading the output

The model hands back two things. First, its best guess at the current state: the single most likely regime right now. Second, the full probability breakdown across all three states, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

A regime call at 90% confidence and one at 40% confidence should not lead to the same decision, even if they're pointing at the same state. That distinction is what actually drives how we think about strategy selection and risk from here.
