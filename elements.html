Hidden Markov Model — Explanation

A clear, intuitive overview of how our regime‑detection model works.

What is a Hidden Markov Model?

A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) is a statistical model used to identify patterns that are not directly observable. In financial markets, this means detecting underlying “regimes” — such as trending, mean‑reverting, or volatile environments — based on observable price behavior.

Instead of trying to guess the market’s state directly, the HMM infers it from the data using probabilities. This makes it extremely useful for understanding market structure and anticipating shifts in behavior.

Why Use an HMM for Markets?

Markets behave differently depending on the underlying regime. Trend‑following works well in trending markets, but poorly in choppy ones. Mean‑reversion thrives in stable environments but fails during breakouts.

The HMM helps identify these conditions by analyzing returns, volatility, or other features and determining which regime the market is most likely in. This allows traders and analysts to adapt their strategies accordingly.

How the Model Works (Intuitive Overview)

The HMM assumes the market is always in one of several hidden states. Each state has its own characteristics — for example, low volatility, high volatility, or trending behavior.

The model looks at observable data (like returns) and determines:

  • Which state the market is likely in right now
  • How likely it is to transition to another state
  • How each state typically behaves

Over time, this creates a clear picture of how the market moves between regimes.

States and Regimes

In our implementation, each state represents a distinct market condition. For example:

  • State 1 — Stable / Low Volatility
  • State 2 — Trending / Directional
  • State 3 — High Volatility / Chaotic

The model learns these states automatically from the data — we do not manually define them. This makes the model flexible and adaptive to different market environments.

Training the Model

The HMM is trained on historical market data. During training, it learns:

  • How often each state occurs
  • How likely the market is to move from one state to another
  • What each state “looks like” in terms of returns or volatility

Once trained, the model can analyze new data and determine the current regime in real time.

Interpreting the Output

The model produces two key outputs:

  • Most likely current state — the regime the market is in right now
  • State probabilities — how likely each regime is at the moment

These outputs help guide strategy selection, risk management, and market interpretation.

What’s Next?

This explanation page will expand as we add more models and refine our HMM implementation. Future updates may include visualizations, interactive charts, and deeper breakdowns of regime behavior.
